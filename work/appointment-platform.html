Healthcare · Patient messaging · Majesco · 2020

Appointment Reminder Platform - one place for a medical practice to remind, check in and reach every patient.

A web application for medical practices that sends appointment reminders by text, email and voice, tracks who has arrived and checked in, broadcasts messages to patient groups, and reports on every transmission. I designed the platform end to end in Adobe XD at Majesco - the navigation model, five modules, the responsive tablet layout and the component set behind them.

Summary

Mission

A busy practice loses money every time a patient forgets an appointment, and loses time every time the front desk has to phone people one by one. The platform's job was to automate the reminding - text, email and recorded voice - and then close the loop: know when a patient has arrived, get the check-in form and payment done before they reach the desk, send recalls and bill reminders to the right group, and show the practice what actually went out and what came back. All of it had to be usable by a receptionist between phone calls, on a desktop or a tablet at the counter.

My contributions

I owned the product design from structure to final UI. I started with the navigation model - a slim icon rail with five modules (Appointment, Arrived, Broadcast, Reports, Upload) plus search, notifications and account - so every screen shares one frame. Then the flows inside each module: the appointment list with a messaging-status language, the arrival board with a side panel for check-in and payment, the broadcast composer for text, email and voice with templates and scheduling, group and contact management with CSV upload, and the three report views. I built the master component set the screens are made from, and the tablet layout that collapses the rail into a menu.

Impact

Delivered as a complete high-fidelity Adobe XD design with a working click-through prototype: five modules, every dialog and popup state, the notification panel and the tablet breakpoint, all assembled from one master component set so the development team could build from a single source.

Timeline

2020

One rail, five modules, one status language.

Everything hangs off a slim icon rail on the left: Appointment, Arrived, Broadcast, Reports and Upload, with search, notifications and the account at the top and bottom. Badges on the rail icons carry counts, so a receptionist can see two patients waiting or three new reminders without opening anything. Inside, every list uses the same row and the same set of status pills - Noting Scheduled, Pending, In Progress, Complete, Confirmed, Need Attention, Received Too Late - so a status means the same thing on every screen.

Decision

Counts live on the rail

A badge on the Arrived icon that says 2 is the whole point of the product for a receptionist. The rail is always visible, so the count is too.

Decision

One row component everywhere

Appointments, arrivals, broadcasts and search results all use the same row: date and time on top, patient name large, a status pill on the right. Learn it once.

Arrival: from car park to doctor ready without the desk.

When a patient marks themselves as arrived, the practice sees it on the Arrived board and a side panel tracks what has to happen next: arrival confirmed, check-in form completed, payment requested and paid, insurance checked, doctor ready. Each step shows its time and a link to the form or receipt. Reminders and reschedule requests appear on the appointment detail so the desk never has to look them up.

Decision

Check-in as a timeline, not a form

Arrival, check-in form, payment, insurance, doctor ready - each step with a tick, a time and a link. The desk sees at a glance which step a waiting patient is stuck on.

Decision

Details in a side panel

Patient details open beside the list, not on a new page, so the receptionist keeps the board in view while handling one person.

Broadcast: one composer for text, email and voice.

A broadcast starts from a group, then the practice ticks the channels it wants - text, email, voice - and composes each one in a tab. Text has a character counter and templates; email has a branded template with the practice header; voice can be recorded by phone with step-by-step instructions, or online with a waveform preview. Send now or schedule for a date and time. Groups come from a CSV upload that reports how many contacts loaded and how many had warnings.

Decision

Channels as tabs, not three screens

Tick text, email and voice once, then compose each in a tab under the same group header. One broadcast, one send button, three channels.

Decision

Voice for people who will never record online

The phone-in instructions sit beside the composer: dial the number, enter the account and access codes, follow the prompts. It is the path most practice managers actually use.

Reports, and the tablet at the counter.

Reports has three tabs. Transmissions lists every message with its status, file size and record count. Campaigns covers recalls and bill reminders. Analytics turns the patient satisfaction survey into totals, response rates and a per-provider score table. On a tablet the rail becomes a menu button and every list, panel and composer reflows to a single column.

What I'd carry into the next operations product.

  • Decide the status vocabulary first. Seven pills, named and coloured once, made every list in the product readable without a legend.
  • Keep the board in view. Side panels for details meant the receptionist never lost the queue while handling one patient.
  • Design for the least technical path. Recording a voice message by phone, with the steps printed on screen, mattered more than the online recorder.
  • Build the master components before the screens. One row, one chip, one upload state - and the tablet layout was a reflow, not a redesign.

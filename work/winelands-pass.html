Upwork · Freelance · Mobile app · 2023 · ★ 5.0 client rating

Winelands Pass — one pass, a weekend of wine country, all in your pocket.

A consumer app for South Africa's Cape Winelands: buy a day, week or season pass, browse discounted tastings, dining, stays, shopping and experiences across the region's towns, and redeem at the venue with a QR code. An Upwork engagement for a South African client: the brief was "an app like GrabOne, but your own take" — I designed it from wireframes to hi-fidelity UI for iOS and Android.

Summary

Mission

The client came to Upwork with a clear reference and an open brief: GrabOne, the New Zealand deals app, was the model — same idea, but for the Cape Winelands, and with my own take on how it should look and work. The only fixed constraint was the colour palette, taken from the client's existing website. Winelands Pass bundles the region's estates, restaurants, guest houses and experiences into one membership: buy a pass, get member pricing everywhere it's accepted, show a QR code at the door. The app had to sell the pass to someone who has never heard of it, and then be the thing they open twenty times over a weekend.

My contributions

I designed the app end to end for the client — greyscale wireframes for every screen first, then the full UI: onboarding, social and email sign-up, the Explore home with category rails and trending listings, Deals & Offers with sort and filters, listing detail with call / directions / share / review, the three-step "Buy your PASS" flow, the My PASS wallet with the QR code, profile, notifications and the filter drawer. The brand — the WP monogram, the warm sand-and-terracotta palette, a serif display face over a clean sans — was carried from the client's identity into a usable mobile system.

Impact

The hi-fidelity visual design milestone was approved on submission and the contract closed with a 5.0 rating. The wireframe-first approach meant the client signed off structure before seeing colour, so the UI phase went through with very few structural changes.

The product in one line: buy once, save everywhere.

GrabOne sells individual deals; Winelands Pass sells a membership that unlocks them. That difference shaped everything. Every screen had to reinforce three ideas — the pass has value, the deals are real, and redeeming is effortless — which gave the app its four tabs: Explore, Deals & Offers, My PASS, My Profile.

VALUE

Show the saving on every card

"Save ZAR 14 with WP" and the struck-through regular price appear on every listing, so the pass keeps justifying itself as you browse.

CHOICE

Deals you can sort and filter

Latest arrival, travel, beauty & spa, food & drink — plus a filter drawer by city, event type, calendar, discount and group type.

REDEEM

The pass is a QR card

My PASS shows the active pass as a branded card — name, type, validity dates and a big QR code the venue scans. Order history sits one tab away.

The app on one board.

Before wireframes, a flow chart the client could agree on: launch → sign up or sign in (Apple, Google, Facebook, or a manual form with email verification) → home, with a location permission on first run. From home, eight destinations — Experiences, Deal Listing, Search, Merchant Centre, Cart, About, User Profile and Customer Care — and the sub-flows under Experiences and Profile that became the app's screens.

Decision

Let people skip sign-in

The "Skipped" branch goes straight to Home. Browsing deals shouldn't need an account; buying the pass does.

Decision

Experiences as the front door

"What are you looking for?", featured deals, WP passes, how-to and testimonials all hang off Experiences — which is why the Explore tab opens with that exact question.

Structure first, in greyscale.

Every screen was wireframed before any colour was applied, so the client could argue about hierarchy and copy without being distracted by the brand. The pass selector, the saving on every card, the four-tab bar and the Call / Direction / Share / Post actions were all settled here.

Wireframe → final UI

What changed was tone, not structure. The greyscale Explore says "Nearby, Food & Dining, Entertainment"; the final says "Tastings, Dining, Stay" because the client wanted the wine-country identity in the words, not only the colours. Listing, notifications and profile kept their layouts almost exactly.

First run — sell the idea, then ask for an account.

The splash and onboarding carry the brand illustration and explain the pass in three swipes before any form appears. Sign-up leads with Apple, Google and Facebook; email is the fallback, not the default.

Explore and deals — browsing that keeps the saving in view.

Dark header, light content: search, notifications and the category rail sit on charcoal, the listings get the white space. Every card carries the rating, the "Save with WP" line and both prices. Below the trending carousel the home screen sells the pass itself, then new offers, then a how-to.

Buying and carrying the pass.

Three steps — personal details, what's printed on the pass, order summary — with a progress bar in the header, then a saved-cards wallet for next time. The result lives in My PASS as a card the venue can scan.

Decision

The pass looks like a pass

A branded card with the holder's name and dates, not a settings row. It's the thing people show at a counter, so it had to feel like a ticket and be readable from arm's length. Day, Week, Month and Year each get their own colour.

Decision

Lean on the platform

Apple ID sign-in, the iOS share sheet and native call dialogs are used as-is. Custom versions would have cost trust and time for no gain.

A small design system, built as the screens were.

Every repeating element lives on a Components page as a variant set, so the 70-odd screens in the final file are assembled from a short list of parts. Nothing on a screen is a one-off.

Type pairs a serif display face for headings and pass names with a clean sans for everything else — the same contrast as the WP monogram. Four elevation levels cover cards, drawers and dialogs.

Also in the library: search (default, focused, filled), favourite toggle, check states (unchecked, checked, mixed), a six-step rating component, read / unread notification rows, a list item, the pass segment tab, the trending card, and a pass component used across My PASS and checkout.

What I took from it.

  • Wireframe every screen, not just the key ones. The screens that cause trouble late are the "boring" ones — profile, notifications, filters. Wireframing all of them kept the UI phase clean.
  • Put the value proposition on the card. A membership product has to keep proving itself. Showing the saving on every listing did more for the pass than any onboarding copy.
  • Brand lives in the words too. "Tastings, Dining, Stay" did as much for the wine-country feel as the palette did.
  • Native where it matters. Sign-in, sharing and calling are moments of trust; using the OS's own UI for them is a design decision, not a shortcut.
